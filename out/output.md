## 0. [Disentangling Forgetting from Utility: Lightweight LLM Unlearning via Orthogonal Null-Space Projection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17864) <a id="link0"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.17864
**Authors:** Xiuyuan Wang, Weiming Liu, Zongxin Yang, Fan Wang, Liemiao Jin, Rubin Qin, Wei Chen, Bin Shi, Chaochao Chen, Xiaolin Zheng

**Abstract:** arXiv:2611.17864v1 Announce Type: new  Abstract: Large Language Models (LLMs) pose significant security risks by retaining private or hazardous information, creating an urgent need for unlearning techniques that excise specific knowledge without compromising general utility. However, prevailing fine-tuning-based unlearning methods struggle to achieve this balance, often suffering from computational inefficiency and unintended utility degradation due to the entangled nature of knowledge within model parameters. To disentangle forgetting from utility, we propose UUE, a theoretically grounded framework that reformulates unlearning as controllable model editing. First, UUE projects parameter updates into the orthogonal left null space of retained knowledge representations, strictly ensuring that the editing process causes minimal interference to the model's general capabilities. Second, distinct from targeted mapping, we introduce a convex untargeted objective that derives a closed-form analytical solution to effectively disrupt undesired knowledge by pushing forget-set embeddings toward their antipodal points. We implement UUE via lightweight, pluggable adapters (including a low-rank variant, UUE-L), enabling efficient deployment without modifying the frozen backbone. Extensive experiments on TOFU and WMDP benchmarks demonstrate that UUE and UUE-L achieve superior unlearning efficacy, significantly outperforming existing methods.

**Comment:** Matches criterion 9: Proposes a new theoretically grounded method for lightweight unlearning in LLMs via orthogonal null-space projection, effectively removing specific knowledge while preserving utility.

## 1. [Two-Clustering Regime of Token Dynamics in Causal Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18088) <a id="link1"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.18088
**Authors:** Trinh T. Nguyen, Duy-Tung Pham, Hoang-Son Do, Tan Minh Nguyen, Thieu N. Vo

**Abstract:** arXiv:2611.18088v1 Announce Type: new  Abstract: We partially resolve Conjecture 1 of [Karagodin et al., Clustering in Causal Attention Masking (NeurIPS 2024)] and extend it to time-dependent parameter regimes. The conjecture predicts that causal attention dynamics converge to two clusters aligned with the eigenvectors associated with the largest eigenvalue $\lambda_{\max}$ of the value transformation. We prove that this behavior holds when $\lambda_{\max}>0$ and is generally false when $\lambda_{\max}\le0$. The resulting two-cluster regime is common in practice and substantially more challenging than the classical single-cluster setting studied previously. A central contribution of our work is the identification of a rigorous dimension reduction phenomenon: despite evolving in a high-dimensional embedding space, token trajectories collapse onto a two-dimensional structure governed by a small number of dominant directions. Building on this insight, we introduce a new analytical framework--instability within the flow--inspired by instability phenomena in physical systems. This framework enables the analysis of steady-regime behavior in non-monotone, non-autonomous attention dynamics. Our theoretical findings are illustrated through simulations and experimental studies using Group Query Attention (GQA) layers.

## 2. [Right Token, Right Time: From Per-Token Gating to Trajectory-Aware Activation Steering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18274) <a id="link2"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.18274
**Authors:** Nima Eshraghi, Lovedeep Gondara, Sagarika Suresh, Xiaotong Xu, Jithin Pradeep, Fanny Chevalier

**Abstract:** arXiv:2611.18274v1 Announce Type: new  Abstract: Activation steering via sparse autoencoders (SAEs) enables behavioral control of large language models without task-specific fine-tuning. Yet, standard methods apply the steering signal at every generated token, displacing representations from the distribution the model was trained on and eroding generation quality. We show that \emph{when} along a generation the intervention is applied is a decisive axis of control. We cast steering as a per-token allocation problem under a fixed budget of steerable tokens, so that methods differ only in which tokens they select, making allocation the sole variable. Within this framework we introduce two gates. The first, Contrastive Feature Projection (CFP), is memoryless and selects the tokens least aligned with the target behavior through a sparse, interpretable feature projection. The second, Closed-Loop Adaptive Bernoulli Steering (CLABS), is trajectory aware. It treats allocation as closed-loop feedback control, tracking the alignment a generation has accumulated so far and spending budget where the trajectory falls behind. On a linear steering-response model we derive an explicit gain region that guarantees closed-loop stability of CLABS and show that its integral term reduces the steady-state tracking offset. Empirically, CFP and CLABS achieve the largest behavioral shift among budgeted gates at matched budgets, with CLABS strongest under the tight budgets where allocation matters most, while best preserving fluency.

**Comment:** Matches criterion 7: Presents new methods or improvements for sparse autoencoders in neural network interpretability. The paper explicitly uses sparse autoencoders for activation steering to control LLM behavior without fine-tuning, introducing new gating methods with theoretical guarantees on stability and empirical improvements.

These results suggest that a harness for agentic RL can be learned from the task's data and evolved with the model.

## 8. [See Every Block Once: Variance-Reduced Post-Training for Masked Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17754) <a id="link8"></a>

## 46. [LLM-Guided Cluster Lifecycle Management: Deciding When and How to Evolve Behavioral Clusters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17616) <a id="link46"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.17616
**Authors:** Himanshu Kulkarni, Siddharth Krishnamurthy, Jianhong Xin

**Abstract:** arXiv:2611.17616v1 Announce Type: new  Abstract: Behavioral clusters learned over user or item interactions drift as behavior evolves: some fragment into sub-behaviors, some converge, some disappear, and new patterns emerge. Two paradigms dominate the maintenance of evolving clusterings. Threshold triggers fire a pre-specified action when a hardcoded statistic crosses a cutoff; refit-and-track (MONIC-style) re-clusters every window and infers transitions from how the new clusters align with the previous window's. Both hardwire the decision into a threshold or a re-clustering rule. We study an alternative in which a large language model (LLM) makes the lifecycle decision in place. Given a threshold-free, whole-population view of the current cluster set (statistical health, labels, member texts, residual mass), the LLM decides per cluster whether and how to act (split, merge, retire, create, or no-action) without re-clustering. Because the useful signal channel depends on how informative the labels are, we fix a richness-conditioned policy in advance: statistics when label distinctiveness is low, fused statistics+semantics when it is high. On three public datasets spanning label richness (YooChoose 0.17, MOOC 0.50, News-Category 0.70), the policy-selected LLM mode obtains the highest F1 on all three, with far fewer interventions and stronger restraint than tuned threshold heuristics and a MONIC steelman (paired Wilcoxon p <= 0.003, bootstrap 95% CIs excluding zero). The gain is concentrated more in precision than recall. The useful modality shifts from statistics to semantics/fusion as richness rises. Semantics hurt on YooChoose, fusion is roughly neutral on MOOC, and the semantic channel is required on News. One base prompt template and one fixed policy serve all three datasets, whereas the tuned baseline collapses when moved between them. We treat this shift as a practitioner diagnostic observed on three datasets, not a validated law.
